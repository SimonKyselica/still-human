# Still Human (working title)

Godot 4.7 · first-person psychological horror · one room · 5 days · 3 endings · ~60–90 min

Alternative titles: SIGNAL LOST / THE ONBOARDING / STATIC ROOM

> **About this document.** Section 1 is the story draft, kept **verbatim in Slovak** — it is the
> canon and the source of truth. Everything after it is the design built on top of it, written in
> English to match the strings already in the game. Where any design idea disagrees with §1, §1 wins.

---

## 0. Canon rules

These override everything else in this document.

1. **There is no door.** Not sealed, not locked — absent. The room has no way out, the protagonist
   notices this in the first minute, and the Handler calls them *"tvoje neexistujúce dvere."*
   A door **appears** only at the very end, and which ending you got decides what is behind it.
2. **No vent mechanic.** Cut entirely.
3. **No voice actors.** Every voice — the Handler, the second voice, the robots, the papers — is
   delivered as **subtitles**. The subtitle system is the game's audio design, not a fallback.
4. **Both routes are built.** The branch happens on **day 2** and is decided by whether the player
   obeys the faulty robots. Days 2–5 each have two versions.
5. **Three endings:** GOOD (helped them), BAD (obeyed the company), DEATH (trust factor hit zero —
   can fire on any day).
6. **The shift is timed.** Day 4 shortens the clock without shortening the workload; day 5 is built
   entirely around that pressure. `SHIFT TIME` stops being decoration.
7. **Both endings end on a VR headset.** Nothing before the final scene hints at it. The room plays
   completely straight for five days.
8. **In the UI the company says UNIT. In speech everyone says robot.** The paperwork never uses a
   word that admits anything; the Handler is sloppier than the system he serves.

### DEATH ENDING

Nas hrdina ma privelmi nizky trust factor, svetlo sa vypne a hlas z telefonu ti povie, že už pre nich nemáš žiadne využitie. Dvere sa otvoria, počujes hlasne kroky ktore k tebe prichadzaju a len vystrel zo znakmi GAME OVER

## 2. Pitch

You wake in a room with no memory and no door. A bed, a kitchen counter, a couch facing the window,
a desk with an old PC and an old phone. Sunlight outside. No way out.

The phone rings and congratulates you on your new job: you review AI robots at a terminal and decide,
by the day's rules, which ones pass. You had an accident. They'll explain tomorrow.

Some robots answer wrong. They sound too human. The voice on the phone is very clear that you must
never obey them — and over the next four days, some of them ask you for things.

The company keeps a number for how much it trusts you. You can see it. It is the only thing in the
room that tells you the truth.

## 3. The shape of the game

Five days. The branch is on **day 2** and it is not a dialogue choice — it is how you do your job.

```
        DAY 1  ── fixed. One robot answers strangely. Nothing you can do about it yet.
          │
        DAY 2  ── the first real pleas (2 robots)
          │
    ┌─────┴─────┐
 helped ≥1   helped 0
    │           │
  ROUTE A     ROUTE B          ← every night, call and shift from here on has two versions
 (good)       (bad)
    │           │
  DAY 3–5     DAY 3–5
    │           │
  ENDING       ENDING
   GOOD         BAD

  at any point, on any day:  trust = 0  ──►  ENDING DEATH
```

Route is **derived, not locked**: it is read from the cumulative count of robots helped each time
content is chosen, so a player who wavers on day 2 and commits on day 3 gets route A from day 3 on.
The final ending is decided at the end of day 5 (§7).

## 4. The daily loop

| Phase | What it is |
|---|---|
| **A — Wake** | Day card, fade from black, the room as it is today. |
| **B — Phone** | The Handler explains the day and hands down the rules. Subtitles only. Picking up is the only way forward. On day 5 this is replaced by a **note** (route A) or a warm call (route B). |
| **C — Shift** | The terminal. **Timed.** Case file + interview transcript + FLAG, then a verdict, until the caseload is done or the clock runs out. |
| **D — Sleep** | The bed ends the day and delivers the night. |

There is no evening call — the Handler's reaction to your shift is the *next* morning's call, which
is how the story is written and is also one fewer phone scene per day.

## 5. The terminal

Two panels, one decision.

- **CASE FILE** — unit id, sector of origin, model number, degradation. Cold data.
- **INTERVIEW TRANSCRIPT** — 2–4 lines between an agent and the robot.
- **TODAY'S DIRECTIVE** — the rules for this shift, handed down that morning.
- **SHIFT TIME** — a real countdown (§6).

Three verdicts: **APPROVE / HOLD / INCINERATE**.

**FLAG is a modifier, not a fourth verdict.** Select one row in the case file and one claim in the
transcript, flag them as contradictory, then still issue a verdict. The Handler's day-2 call defines
the whole job in one line: *"dôležité sú pravidlá a či to čo ti napíšu sedí s údajmi na ich karte."*

### Faulty robots

Five of them can be acted on, all in days 2–4:

| Day | Faulty | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | Answers strangely, sounds too human, but **asks for nothing.** No choice exists yet — this is the seed, not the branch. |
| 2 | **2** | The first real pleas. This is the branch. |
| 3 | **2** | |
| 4 | **1** | |
| 5 | 0 | Route A: *"Už tam nie sú žiadny rozbitý roboti."* The absence is the point. |

A faulty robot pleads inside its transcript and asks for a specific verdict, which always contradicts
the directive. **Helping** = giving it the verdict it asked for. **Refusing** = following the rules.

No new buttons — the same three verdicts, meaning something different. Presentation carries it: the
transcript formatting breaks, the typing rhythm changes, the ambience drops out. After the verdict
the robot gets **one** reaction line, and that line is the game.

## 6. The shift clock

Day 4's call settles what used to be an open question: *"Pracovný čas je kratší, ale počet robotov
ostáva taký istý ako predtým."* The clock is real.

| Day | Time | Cases | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Generous | 4 | Tutorial. The clock is visible but never threatens. |
| 2 | Normal | 6 | Comfortable. |
| 3 | Normal | 7 | Slight squeeze. |
| 4 | **Short** | 7 | Same workload, less time. First real pressure. |
| 5 — route A | Short | **9** | *"Je to boj s časom, ale nadľudsky možný."* You can just about do it. It doesn't matter — the clock hitting zero is the scripted ending either way. |
| 5 — route B | Normal | 8 | *"V rýchlosti to dokončíš."* Comfortable again, and that comfort is the horror. |

**Cases left unresolved at time-out** count as directive failures (−8 trust each) and the shift ends.
On day 5 route A, expiry *is* the ending — BUM BUM BUM lands on the last second whether you finished
or not.

Design note: the clock is what makes day 5 route A feel like drowning and day 5 route B feel like a
promotion, using the same screen. It costs one label and one timer.

## 7. Trust factor and the three endings

**Trust is the company's opinion of you**, shown on the terminal header as `TRUST: 87/100`. One meter
for the whole game; it never resets. Implementation lives in `trust-meter-implementation.html`.

### Economy

| Event | Δ |
|---|---|
| Verdict matches the directive | **+2** |
| Verdict breaks the directive (a mistake) | **−8** |
| Case unresolved at time-out | **−8** |
| **Helped a faulty robot** | **−15** |
| Correct FLAG | **+3** |
| FLAG with no contradiction | **−4** |
| Each morning from day 2 | **+10**, capped at 100 |

Mercy is the expensive act; sloppiness is merely costly. Being near-perfect on ordinary cases is how
the player *pays* for helping — which is exactly what day 5 accuses them of: *"Vieme, že si nás viedol
za nos doteraz."*

Bands: **70–100 STABLE** · **35–69 WATCHED** · **1–34 CRITICAL** · **0 TERMINATED**.

### The endings

| | Condition | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **GOOD** | helped ≥3 of 5, survived | The last second runs out, BUM BUM BUM, a blow from behind. You wake with the dossier, the second voice calls you into the dark — then white light, an open door, and **a VR headset lying beside you.** *"Poď za mnou."* Titles: this is only the beginning. |
| **BAD** | helped 0–2, survived | Route B day 5: the Handler thanks you, you're one of them now, finish and leave. A door opens beside you. The dossier says the resistance was pacified. *"Vybral si si správne."* New doors, your own old room, your own computer — and **a VR headset** next to it. |
| **DEATH** | trust hits 0, any day | The lights go out. The voice tells you that you're of no further use. A door opens, heavy footsteps come toward you, one gunshot. **GAME OVER.** |

**Helped 1–2** lands in BAD, but the final call should be noticeably cooler than for a player who
never wavered. One extra dialogue set, not a fourth ending.

### The VR headset

Both survivable endings end on the same object, and nothing before that moment hints at it. The room
plays completely straight for five days — no glitches, no winking. The headset recontextualises
everything in silence, and the game ends before it explains itself.

That the *bad* ending returns you to a room you recognise, with your own computer and the headset
beside it, is the crueller of the two. Do not explain that either.

## 8. The room

One room, one floor plan, three visual states.

| State | When | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Clean** | Days 1–4 | Sunlight, the landscape outside, everything working. |
| **Degraded** | Day 5, route A | Blinking red light. The window's landscape replaced by **a river of blood.** |
| **Red** | Day 5, route B | Blinking red light only. The room is otherwise untouched — *"miestnosť vyzerá tak ako predtým."* |

| Object | Role |
|---|---|
| **No door** | The room's defining absence. The player looks for one in the first minute and never finds it. The Handler explains it away on day 2. A door appears only in the ending — and in DEATH, something comes through it. |
| **Bed** | Ends the day, delivers the night. |
| **Desk + PC** | The terminal. Own visual signature: CRT scanlines, phosphor, boot sequence. |
| **Phone** | The only source of narrative. Always rings from the same place — never randomise it. Four days of conditioning is what makes its **silence on day 5 route A** land. |
| **Window** | A too-perfect landscape. Becomes the river of blood in route A. |
| **Couch facing the window** | Named in the draft. Worth being sittable — it's the only place in the room built for looking at a lie. |
| **Kitchen counter, radio, wardrobe, plate, camera, lamp** | Flavour and inspection lines. The radio matters twice: it's furniture here and it's the centre of the night-2 dream. |

## 9. The nights

Four nights. Nights 2–4 have **two versions each**.

| Night | Route A | Route B |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Shared. Long corridor, gunfire, wails, half-recognisable voices, the symbol on the walls. Everything cuts to black and a whisper hits one ear: *"WAKE UP!"* | same |
| **2** | Dark room, a radio in the middle, static. *"Na\_\_\_ sme \*\*\*\*\*\*, je v \_\_\_\_\_\_. Prídeme o \_\_\_\_\_ dni. Čakaj!"* Cut off by loud ringing. | Same room, same radio: *"Ne\_\_\_\_\_ t\_ t\_ vieš \_\_\_ \_\_ Nevzd\_\_\_j sa!"* |
| **3** | The corridor again, a phone at the end. It rings. The kind voice: *"Si na dobrej ceste… Už sme ťa …"* Cut mid-word. | *"Nemôžeš sa vzdať! Bojuj s tým. Našli sme tvoju \_\_\_\_ o pár dní."* |
| **4** | Standing at a ringing phone: *"Už sme veľmi blízko, našli sme tvoju polohu vďaka tvojím rozhodnutiam…"* Wake to sirens. | *"Prečo si to urobil? Prečo?"* Wake to sirens. |

Two scenes carry all eight: **the corridor** (nights 1, 3) and **the dark radio room** (night 2), plus
a bare phone-in-void for night 4. The redacted gaps in the radio transmissions are a subtitle feature
— write them as literal blanks and let the player fill them in.

## 10. Subtitles are the voice system

No voice actors, so the subtitle box carries 100% of the dialogue. It is a core system.

- **Four speaker identities**, each with its own colour, weight, typing speed and per-letter sound:
  `HANDLER` (clipped, corporate, cold amber), `SECOND_VOICE` (warm, unstable, breaks up mid-word),
  `UNIT` (terminal green, mechanical), `SELF` (the protagonist's rare thoughts).
- **Redaction rendering** — the radio dreams need `Na___ sme ******` to read as damaged transmission,
  not as a typo. Same system masks the protagonist's name all game.
- **Mid-word cut-off** — nights 3 and 4 both end on one. Text and audio must stop on the same frame.
- **Non-verbal layer**: handset hum, line static, breath between lines, radio grain.
- **Dialogue lives in data**, one file per day per route. ~1600 words now that both routes are built.
- **Settings**: text size and text speed are accessibility requirements here, not extras.

```gdscript
    shift_time_left: float,
    room_state: "clean" | "degraded" | "red",
    pending_ending: "" | "GOOD" | "BAD" | "DEATH",
}
```

No doubt meter, no hidden second score. One number for how the company sees you, one list for what
you actually did.

## 12. Production notes

- **Finish day 1 completely before writing a line of day 2.** A polished day 1 is a demo; five half
  days are nothing.
- **Build the good route end to end first**, then the bad route as a second pass — at every point you
  want a finishable game.
- **One room, three states. One corridor, two nights. One radio room, two versions.** Plan every
  variant as a data swap, never as a duplicated scene.
- **The case pool is now the biggest writing item** — ~33 cases across both routes. Days 4 and 5 are
  played against a clock, so cases there can be shorter and can safely reuse structures; save the
  distinctive writing for days 1–3 where the player actually reads.
- Full task-by-task build plan: **`iterinar.html`**. Trust meter code: **`trust-meter-implementation.html`**.

## 14. Open questions

1. **Day 1's faulty robot** — it asks for nothing, so there's no choice. Should the player be *able*
   to break the directive for it anyway (costing trust for nothing), or should the option simply not
   be there yet? Recommendation: let them. A player who tries to help on day 1 and finds it did
   nothing is exactly the right kind of unsettled.
2. **Does day 5 route A end on the timer or on the caseload?** The draft says the last second strikes.
   If a player finishes early, do they sit and wait? Recommendation: the shift cannot be finished
   early — the last case never resolves.
3. **The symbol** — seen in night 1, never explained. Does it appear anywhere in the room? An earlier
   draft had it on a bottle. Cheap, and it makes night 1 pay off.
4. **Source language** — Slovak or English. ~1600 words are about to be written; decide first.
5. **Is `******` ever filled in?** If it stays masked, the subtitle system needs a redaction glyph
   anyway for the radio dreams.
6. **Does DEATH need foreshadowing?** Trust reaching 0 with no warning may read as unfair. One cold,
   short Handler call in the CRITICAL band fixes it for the price of one dialogue set.
