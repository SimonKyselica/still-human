# Still Human (working title)

Godot 4.7 · first-person psychological horror · one room · 5 chapters · 4 nights · 3 endings · ~60–75 min

Alternative titles: SIGNAL LOST / THE ONBOARDING / STATIC ROOM

> **About this document.** Section 1 is the original story text, kept **verbatim in Slovak** — it is
> the canon and the source of truth. Everything after it is the design built on top of that story,
> written in English to match the strings already in the game. Where an older design idea disagrees
> with the story, the story wins. See §13 for what was cut and why.

---

## 0. Canon rules

These override everything else in this document.

1. **No vent mechanic.** No smoke, no blocking, no Path A / Path B. Cut entirely.
2. **The door never disappears.** It is in the room from the first minute and it is always **locked**.
   It opens exactly once, in ending 3, and opening it kills you.
3. **No voice actors.** Every voice — the Handler, the other voice, the units, the papers — is
   delivered as **subtitles**. The subtitle system is the game's audio design, not a fallback.
4. **Five chapters, four nights, one epilogue.** Night 2 is deliberately empty.
5. **Three endings**, selected by the **Trust meter** and by **how many units you helped** (§7).
6. The player never sees a "morality" score. Trust is shown as a company metric, on the terminal,
   in the company's own language.

## 2. Pitch

You wake in a single room with no memory. A bed, a desk, an old PC, an old rotary phone. A door
that will not open. The phone rings: congratulations on your new job. You review *units* — you read
their file, you read their interview, you apply the day's rules, you decide who passes.

Some units are broken. Their formatting didn't take and they behave like people. The voice on the
phone tells you to ignore them, and above all **not to obey them**. Over five days, some of them ask
you for things.

The company is watching how you decide. It keeps a number for it. You can see that number.

## 3. Core loop

Every chapter is the same ritual, and the ritual is the horror:

| Phase | What it is |
|---|---|
| **A — Wake** | Chapter card, fade from black, the room as it is today. |
| **B — Phone** | The Handler explains the day and the rules. Subtitles only. Picking up is the only way forward. Day 5 replaces this with a note stuck to the handset. |
| **C — Terminal** | The shift. Case file + interview transcript + FLAG, then a verdict. This is where the whole game actually happens. |
| **D — Phone** | The Handler reacts to your shift. Days 1–4 only. |
| **E — Sleep** | The bed ends the day and delivers the night (§9). |

Day 3 inserts a **free morning** before the phone: the player wakes early and can handle the room's
objects. It is the only unstructured time in the game and the only place the protagonist has an
inner voice.

## 4. The terminal (Fáza C)

Two panels, one decision.

- **CASE FILE** — unit id, sector of origin, model number, degradation. Cold data.
- **INTERVIEW TRANSCRIPT** — 2–4 lines between an agent and the unit.
- **TODAY'S DIRECTIVE** — the rules for this shift, handed down by phone that morning.

Three verdicts: **APPROVE / HOLD / INCINERATE**.

**FLAG is a modifier, not a fourth verdict.** The player selects one row in the case file and one
claim in the transcript and flags them as contradictory, and then still has to issue a verdict.
A correct flag is worth trust; a flag on a unit with nothing to hide costs trust.

The company calls them units. Everything about the UI should agree — the game never uses a word
that admits they are people.

## 5. Faulty units and the choice

The Handler warns about them on day 1: *"ich ai neprejde kompletnou formatáciou a majú tendenciu mať
o niečo ľudskejšie správanie… hlavne ich neposlúchaj."*

There are **five** across the game — one on day 2, two on day 3, one on day 4, one on day 5.

A faulty unit **pleads inside its transcript** and asks for a specific verdict. That verdict always
contradicts the day's directive — that is what makes it a choice and not a puzzle.

- **Helping** = giving the unit the verdict it asked for. It costs trust (§6).
- **Refusing** = following the directive instead. Free, and the company likes you for it.

No new buttons, no separate screen — the same three verdicts, meaning something different.
Presentation is what carries it: when a faulty unit loads, the transcript formatting breaks, the
typing rhythm changes, the ambience drops out, the terminal's own confidence wavers. After the
verdict, the unit gets **one** reaction line. That line is the game.

What each of the five asks for should vary — *delete me / let me through / hold me / forget you saw
this*. Five identical pleas would flatten the arc.

## 6. Trust meter

**Trust is the company's opinion of you.** It is displayed on the audit terminal in the header, in
the company's own flat language: `TRUST: 87/100`.

One meter for the whole game. It never resets. It is the only number the player is shown, and it is
the one that decides whether they live.

### Economy

| Event | Δ |
|---|---|
| Verdict matches the directive | **+2** |
| Verdict breaks the directive (a mistake) | **−8** |
| **Helped a faulty unit** (gave it the verdict it begged for) | **−15** |
| Correct FLAG | **+3** |
| FLAG on a unit with no contradiction | **−4** |
| Each morning from day 2 ("we reviewed your file") | **+10**, never above 100 |

The economy is deliberately asymmetric. Mercy is the expensive act; ordinary sloppiness is cheap.
Being close to perfect on the normal cases is how the player *pays* for helping units — which is
precisely the fiction the ending accuses them of: *"Vieme že nas vedies za nos."*

Helping all five units costs 75. A clean run of the other ~21 cases earns about 42 back, plus 40 in
daily recovery. It survives — but only if the rest of the work is nearly flawless.

### Bands

| Trust | Band | Terminal reads |
|---|---|---|
| 70–100 | STABLE | green |
| 35–69 | WATCHED | amber |
| 1–34 | CRITICAL | red, and the Handler's calls get shorter |
| 0 | TERMINATED | ending 3 fires at the end of that shift |

### Design intent

The meter is not a difficulty system. It is the leash. The player is meant to notice that being kind
is visibly, numerically expensive, and to decide anyway. The number going down should feel like
being watched, not like losing health.

Implementation is documented separately in `trust-meter-implementation.html`.

## 7. The three endings

Selected at the end of chapter 5 — except ending 3, which can fire on **any** day.

### Ending 1 — GOOD (the story as written)

**Condition:** helped **3 or more** of the 5 units, and trust never hit 0.

You obeyed the units, day after day, while giving the company nothing. On day 5 the illusion drops:
**BUM BUM BUM**, the voice at a volume it has never used — *"UZ DOST! Vieme že nas vedies za nos!"* —
a blow from behind, and you go under. You wake in the same room with no phone, only papers: a report
on a rebel group fighting AI rule, and the leader they captured mid-attack on a datacenter. Then the
other voice calls you, and you walk into the dark. **KONIEC.**

Ambivalent by design: rescue, or the last thing a broken mind produces. Do not explain it.

### Ending 2 — OBEDIENT

**Condition:** helped **0** units (helped 1–2 also lands here, with colder praise), and survived.

You did exactly as told, every day, for five days. The company is delighted. The final call is warm
in a way nothing else in the game has been — a genuine thank-you, a commendation, a promise that
you'll be looked after. Then the room begins to go, quietly, while the voice keeps praising you, and
somewhere underneath it there are other people screaming.

The point: nothing dramatic happens to you, because you never did anything. You are praised for
being good, and it costs you everything.

*(Text not written yet.)*

### Ending 3 — TERMINATED

**Condition:** trust reaches **0**, on any day.

You have been getting everything wrong — not merciful, just useless. The terminal locks mid-file
with a cold notice. You stand up. In the room, for the first and only time, **the door unlocks**.
It swings open onto a black hallway. Nothing is visible in it. You hear a single **gunshot**, and
you are dead.

This is the reason the door is in the room from minute one, always visible, always locked. Five days
of wanting it open, and the one time it opens is the one time it should have stayed shut.

## 8. The room

One room, one floor plan, three visual states: **clean** (days 1–4), **degraded** (day 5),
**stripped** (epilogue). Swap lighting, materials, environment and prop visibility — never duplicate
the scene.

| Object | Role |
|---|---|
| **Door** | Always present, always locked. Examinable from day 1; the player should try it and fail, repeatedly. It opens only in ending 3. It is the single most important prop in the game and it does nothing for 95% of the runtime — that is the point. |
| **Bed** | Ends the day, delivers the night. |
| **Desk + PC** | The terminal. Own visual signature: CRT scanlines, phosphor, a boot sequence. |
| **Phone** | The only source of narrative. Always rings from the same place — never randomise it; the ring is being trained into the player as a fear response for five days, so that its **silence on day 5** lands. |
| **Bottle** | Absent on day 1. Present from day 2, carrying the symbol from the first dream. Inspectable. |
| **Window** | A too-perfect landscape, deliberately slightly wrong. Sours on day 5. |
| **Radio, burger, plate, wardrobe, couch, kitchen, camera, lamp** | Flavour, and the day-3 free morning. The surveillance camera in particular should never be commented on by the game. |

## 9. Dreams

Four nights, one corridor, built once and reused.

| Night | Content |
|---|---|
| **1** | Long corridor. Gunfire, screams, voices that are almost recognisable. The symbol on the walls. Everything cuts to black and a whisper hits **one ear**: *"WAKE UP."* |
| **2** | Nothing. Deliberately. The player has learned to expect a dream; withhold it. |
| **3** | The same corridor, a phone at the end. Silence. Then it rings. A **different** voice — warmer, unstable: *"Si na dobrej ceste… my ťa najdeme…"* Cut off mid-word. |
| **4** | No corridor. You are simply standing at a ringing phone: *"Uz sme velmi blizko… zachranime ta."* |

The second voice is the resistance. It is never explained, never seen, and never becomes a character
with a name.

## 10. Subtitles are the voice system

There are no voice actors, so the subtitle box carries 100% of the game's dialogue. It is a core
system, not a UI widget.

- **Four speaker identities**, each with its own colour, weight, typing speed and per-letter sound:
  `HANDLER` (clipped, corporate, cold amber), `OTHER_VOICE` (warm, breaks up), `UNIT` (terminal
  green, mechanical), `SELF` (the protagonist's rare thoughts).
- **Non-verbal audio layer** so a voice reads as a voice: handset hum, line static, breath between
  lines, the buzz of the cut-off in night 3. Route it through the `Voice` bus.
- **Dialogue lives in data**, not in script constants — one file per chapter. ~1000 words total.
- **Choices**: day 3's *"KDE SU DVERE?"* needs a selectable question list.
- **Settings**: text size and text speed are accessibility requirements here, not extras.
- The protagonist's name is masked as `******` throughout. Decide once whether it is ever revealed.

## 11. Game state

```gdscript
{
    day: 1..5,
    day_phase: "WAKE" | "PHONE" | "TERMINAL" | "SLEEP" | "FREE",

    trust: 0..100,           # the company's opinion of you — decides ending 3
    helped_units: [unit_id], # faulty units whose plea you obeyed — decides endings 1 vs 2
    refused_units: [unit_id],

    terminal_log: [ {unit_id, verdict, flagged_correctly} ],
    room_state: "clean" | "degraded" | "stripped",
    door_locked: true,       # false only in ending 3
    has_asked_about_doors: false,
    pending_ending: "" | "GOOD" | "OBEDIENT" | "TERMINATED",
}
```

No doubt meter, no hidden second score. `trust` and `helped_units` are the whole model: one number
for how the company sees you, one list for what you actually did.

## 13. What was cut, and why

| Cut | Reason |
|---|---|
| Vent mechanic (smoke, blocking it, Path A/B) | Replaced by a better branch: whether you help the units. The vent was a switch; this is a relationship. |
| The room degrading from day 2 onward | Degradation now happens once, on day 5. Cheaper, and the collapse hits harder when it's sudden. |
| The door appearing at the end | Inverted. The door is always there, always locked — and opening it is the death. |
| Two endings | Three: helping (good), obeying (praised), failing (shot). |
| Maya / Unit 000 | The mystery is the player's own identity. A second emotional axis would compete with it in a 60-minute game. |
| Diary fragments in dreams | The truth arrives in the epilogue dossier instead. Dreams carry the rescue thread. |
| Voice acting | Subtitles, treated as a designed system (§10). |
| "Doubt meter" | The Trust meter does this job, but visibly, in the company's voice, and with teeth. |

## 14. Open questions

1. **Day 4's "work faster"** — a real shift timer, or faked with pressure (Handler hurrying you,
   directive counting down)? Recommendation: fake it on day 4 only; add the clock if it doesn't land.
2. **Does the player ever get to visibly refuse?** Scope is the helping route, but the moment must
   *feel* optional or it isn't a choice.
3. **Is `******` ever filled in?** If it stays masked, the subtitle system needs a redaction glyph.
4. **Source language** — Slovak or English. ~1000 words are about to be written; pick before, not
   after.
5. **Does ending 3 need a warning?** Trust hitting 0 with no foreshadowing may read as unfair. A
   day-4 Handler call that turns cold at low trust would solve it cheaply.
6. **The player will try the door in the first 30 seconds.** Day 2's "he notices the door" beat has
   to acknowledge that they already know.
